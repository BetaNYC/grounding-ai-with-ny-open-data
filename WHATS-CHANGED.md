# What's changed

This page lists the changes to Grounding AI with NY Open Data that a reader would notice: new guides, demo scripts and slide decks, new data in the directory of New York's data portals, and corrections when we published something wrong. Newest first.

- **History begins:** 2026-07-06, the day the repository started on GitHub. The website opened on 2026-07-30; entries before that date describe the guides as they were on GitHub. Every correction we know of since 2026-07-06 is listed.
- **Data refresh:** none on a schedule. The directory of data portals and the figures in the guides are checked by hand, and each guide says the date its figures were run.

To report a mistake or ask for a new feature, use the [feedback page](https://betanyc.github.io/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/feedback.html) or [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/BetaNYC/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/issues).

## 2026-11-05: Eight portal links went to another state

- **Tags:** Data, Correction
- **What changed:** In the list of New York open data portals, eight county rows linked to a portal in another state with a similar name, from July 6 to November 5, 2026: Delaware, Greene, Monroe and Seneca linked to Ohio, Franklin to Virginia, Montgomery to Maryland, Orange to California and Wyoming to Pennsylvania. All eight are fixed, and Monroe now links to its own New York portal. Every link in the list was checked again on November 5.
- **If you used the old numbers:** If you used the list for any of those eight counties before November 5, 2026, the data you found came from the other state. Use the link in the list now. The other rows were not affected by this mistake.
- **Who it affects:** Anyone who used the portal list for those eight counties.
- **More detail:** [The portal list](https://betanyc.github.io/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/resources/ny-open-data-portals.html) · [The fix](https://github.com/BetaNYC/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/pull/25)

## 2026-10-19: Two decks named the wrong section of the Administrative Code

- **Tags:** Site, Correction
- **What changed:** The CUNY liberal arts deck, from July 29, 2026, and the AI Civics community of practice deck, from September 18, 2026, said Administrative Code section 2-102 names the official animals of New York City. It describes the City seal, which shows two beavers; no section of the Code names an official city animal. The CUNY deck's closing slide also listed one of BetaNYC's own Council awards for FY2026, $25,000, where BetaNYC's FY2026 Council funding was $190,000 in all; it now lists all of it.
- **If you used the old numbers:** If you repeated the section 2-102 example from either deck, it is about the City seal, not an official animal. If you used the $25,000 figure as BetaNYC's FY2026 Council funding, use $190,000.
- **Who it affects:** Anyone who saw or reused these two decks between July 29 and October 19, 2026.
- **More detail:** [The fix](https://github.com/BetaNYC/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/pull/19)

## 2026-07-22: Four claims in the State Senate District 59 demo were wrong

- **Tags:** Site, Correction
- **What changed:** The demo script for State Senate District 59, added the day before, made four wrong claims. It said District 59 is missing from a broadband file because the file is older than the district; the district existed then, and it is missing because the file covers New York City districts only. It also misdescribed what one connector setting and one result field do, and presented a broadband bill as an answer to a prompt it did not answer.
- **If you used the old numbers:** If you repeated the script's explanation of the broadband file, the file covers New York City districts only. The script now describes the setting and the field as they work, and labels the broadband bill as the result of a separate search.
- **More detail:** [The fix](https://github.com/BetaNYC/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/pull/5)

## 2026-07-21: Four figures in the Council district demos were wrong

- **Tags:** Data, Correction
- **What changed:** For about 12 hours on July 21, 2026, the demo scripts for Council Districts 4 and 10 gave discretionary funding totals that were too low, and a list of budget rescissions stopped at 50 rows. Two of the totals came from asking for fewer rows than there were. District 4 had 77 awards, $1,538,000, not 40 awards, $1,148,000; District 10 had 105 awards, $2,165,000, not 12 awards, $960,000.
- **If you used the old numbers:** Use 77 awards and $1,538,000 for District 4, 105 awards and $2,165,000 for District 10, and 4 awards and $90,000, not 2 and $50,000, for District 10's digital equity awards. When a count equals the number of rows you asked for, ask for more before you trust it.
- **More detail:** [The fix](https://github.com/BetaNYC/grounding-ai-with-ny-open-data/pull/4)
