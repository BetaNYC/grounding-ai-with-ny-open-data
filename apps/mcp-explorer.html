NYS Legislation · does Albany intersect?

231,194 bills and 1,992 members from the New York State Senate's Open Legislation API. The question worth asking here is not "what is the state doing" but "has the state tried this before, and what happened."

The intersection: broadband access

New York City enacted a home broadband access law. Albany has been introducing one since 2009.

New York City
Int 1122-2024
A plan for expanding home access to broadband internet. Committee on Technology. Enacted 2025-11-08. 28 sponsors including De La Rosa, who chairs that committee.

Same title, same summary, four sessions, two sponsors. All four carry signed: false, adopted: false, vetoed: false. All four sat in Corporations, Authorities and Commissions. A7509-2025 is not dead — it was re-referred on 2026-01-07 and is pending.
Why this is the killer query. Print numbers change every session, so nothing upstream links a bill to its own past lives. "Has this been tried before" is days of research in Albany and one call here.
⚠️ Coverage gaps, checked against corpus.db 2026-07-27. 231,194 bills and 1,992 members, but floor transcripts and hearing transcripts are both 0 rows, and NYS law sections number only 137. The transcript tools exist and reach the live API, but there is no local corpus behind them. Do not demo transcript search. Stick to bills.
The refusal is the thesis, written by the tool. Pass query= instead of term= and it answers: "Unrecognized parameters are rejected rather than ignored, because silently dropping a filter returns real data that answers a different question."

Open Data · the whole portal, two ways

Any dataset on NYC Open Data, via SoQL. This is the widest surface of the five and the one most able to waste your time, so here is the right way and the wrong way side by side.

The right way: a bounded aggregate

Note UNSANITARY CONDITION arrives upper-case while its neighbours are title-case: complaint_type is uncontrolled text, so grouping on it needs normalizing before you compare across years.
🚨 Correction to our own guides. Both the District 10 and Reinvent Albany scripts say to check is_sample and treat the numbers as meaningless if it is true. That advice is wrong. This aggregate returned is_sample: true, and so did a bare count(*) returning a single row, where "sample" cannot mean anything. Both figures are correct and agree with each other. Following the guidance would make a presenter discard a good number. The flag appears to be unconditional.

limit bounds the row count, not the payload. Five rows became half a megabyte because the matched dataset carries polygon geometry and the server inlines it. This is the single most likely way to stall a live demo. Never run a bare catalog search on a projector. Query a known dataset_id with an explicit select instead, as above.
