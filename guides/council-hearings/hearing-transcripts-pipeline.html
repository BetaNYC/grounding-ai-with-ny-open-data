From a Council hearing to an open, speaker-labeled transcript

How BetaNYC turned two NYC Council AI hearings (October 5 and October 16, 2026) into searchable transcripts, panel timelines and word clouds. Scripts do the counting, and people check the meaning.

This is BetaNYC's independent record, not the Council's official transcript.

Deepgram Nova-3 writes the words and groups voices ("speaker 1, speaker 2"). It gets a short list of this hearing's names. lexicon.txt

Who is "speaker 3"? Self-introductions, the chair calling on people, Zoom name labels, captions. The hearing is split into panels too. panels.json

5

people + AIchecked by script

Remove "um"s and false starts, fix spelling. Timestamps and names never change. validate_clean.py

6

Build the hearing folder

scripted

Turns, speakers, captions, talk time, panel timeline. build_hearing.py, build_panels.py

7

Word clouds

scripted

The phrases each group used more than everyone else. build_wordclouds.py

8

Check the record

people + AI

Every name we could source is checked against a public source; the ones we could not confirm are listed in the hearing's known gaps. Every edit that changes meaning is checked against the captions. Two reviews, then a person opens every page.

GitHub Pagespeople

The site goes live. Every page links to a correction form, and fixes go back into the inputs and rebuild.

What we learned: why people stay in the loop

A helpful word list can backfire

A general list of BetaNYC names turned a witness's self-introduction into a BetaNYC staff name. So each hearing now gets its own short list, with a source for every term.

"Better-sounding" is not "right"

Four cleanup edits were undone ("lunch" to "launch", "dictated" to "abdicated"…). Both the machine and the Council's captions disagreed with them. An edit that changes meaning needs a second source.

Never publish a guess about a person

A witness's law firm had been guessed from a garbled word. The guess was right, but it stayed off the page until a public source confirmed it.

Read the frame, not the transcription

Zoom name labels were read from video frames, so the spelling on the page matches the screen.
