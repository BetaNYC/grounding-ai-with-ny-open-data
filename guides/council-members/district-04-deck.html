Act 4 — the sharpest contrast in the deck. Don't cut it.

This is the subtle one, and the strongest. Re-run 2026-10-19, the web search says a Council package makes the limit three months, then that a DOB proposal penalizes sheds up "more than 180 days," then that it can't confirm which is in effect. Resist the urge to say it failed. It's roughly right, it's uncitable, and it's unsure in a way that costs you.

"It told you three months, then 180 days, then said it couldn't confirm either. And look where it got them: news stories, a trade magazine, and company blogs. You cannot put any of those in a letter to DOB."

Then the precision point. The web summary says penalties apply to sheds up "more than 180 days." The statute says something different: penalties begin at the second renewal, and only where work is not in progress, tiered by how long the shed has existed. Roughly 180 days is a blog's paraphrase of a rule whose actual test is the work, not the clock.

"Close enough to sound right in a meeting. Wrong enough to lose the argument when the owner's lawyer shows up."

And the exemption. One- and two-family homes are exempt, as are sheds up for new building, enlargement, or demolition. The search mentions it only as an "earlier" provision it can't confirm still applies. The statute is current. It is exactly the detail a constituent letter has to get right.

⚠️ Charter search is keyword matching, not semantic. sidewalk shed returns 34 results at limit 50 (10 at the default), including Rules § 102-07, the DOB rule on shed permit renewals and penalties. noise nighttime construction and heat season residential temperature both return zero (checked 2026-10-19). Rehearse your queries. Improvising a phrasing in the room will demo an empty result set.

⚠️ Always read the legal disclaimer if anyone treats this as advice. It's research material, not legal advice, and the corpus is current through Local Law 2026/147 (enacted 2026-09-12), Rules through 2026-10-04, not necessarily through today.
