Act 4 — the sharpest contrast in the deck. Don't cut it.

Be scrupulous about the left column here. Re-run 2026-10-19, it is good: the Health Department's FAQ and Health Code chapter, and on intro.nyc the 2017 local law that amended § 17-194. Its summary gets the January 15 report deadline and the Health Department's $500 penalty (doubling on default) right. Do not say it failed.

"The search found good guidance. It didn't find the law itself, the statute's penalties, or the part your constituents can actually use."

The precision point. The search summary never quotes Admin Code § 17-194. The statute sets $200 to $2,000 per violation for failing to inspect, a separate penalty of up to $250 for failing to post the tenant notice, and $200 to $2,000 for an unsafe tank. None of that is in the summary.

And what the guidance leaves out. Subdivision (d) requires the owner to post a notice telling tenants the results are on file and how to request them, with a five-business-day turnaround, and to tell them they can contact the Health Department if the owner doesn't deliver. That is directly usable by a constituent in a building with bad water, and the search summary doesn't mention it.

⚠️ Charter search is keyword matching, not semantic. water tank inspection works. heat hot water residential returns zero, as does noise nighttime construction. Rehearse your queries. Improvising in the room will demo an empty result set. (Checked 2026-07-27; not re-run 2026-10-19.)
