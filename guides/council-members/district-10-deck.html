Act 4 — the sharpest contrast in the deck. Don't cut it.

Be scrupulous about the left column here. Unlike the District 4 sidewalk-shed search, the actual statute IS in these results, at position six on amlegal. Do not say "not one is the law." Say what's true: it's there, ranked below four companies that sell water tank cleaning and inspection.

"The law is in there. It's the sixth result, under four vendors who'd like to sell you an inspection. Your staffer clicks the first one."

Then the precision point, which is the kill shot. The web summary says penalties are "fines starting at $250 per day." The statute says neither. § 17-194(j) sets $200 to $2,000 per violation for failing to inspect, and $250 is the maximum for a different and much smaller offense — failing to post the tenant notice. So the web number is wrong on the floor, wrong on the ceiling, and wrong on the unit.

"It's off by an order of magnitude in both directions, and it turned a per-violation penalty into a daily one. That's the difference between a letter that works and one that gets laughed at."

And what the vendors leave out. Subdivision (d) requires the owner to post a notice telling tenants the results are on file and how to request them, with a five-business-day turnaround. That is directly usable by a constituent in a building with bad water — and it appears in none of the vendor pages.

The web's "most buildings over six stories" is a vendor rule of thumb, not the statute. The law applies to any building with a tank in its drinking water supply system.

⚠️ Charter search is keyword matching, not semantic. water tank inspection works. heat hot water residential returns zero, as does noise nighttime construction. Rehearse your queries. Improvising in the room will demo an empty result set.
