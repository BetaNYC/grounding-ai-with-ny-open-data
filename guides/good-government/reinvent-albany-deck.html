Act 2

"One vendor, 54 separate discretionary decisions by 41 different sponsors, six and a half million dollars, and no single line item anywhere that says so. Assembling this by hand means reading the Schedule C document end to end. That's the afternoon this replaces."

The refinement worth making to this room. Two of the 41 sponsors are the Brooklyn and Queens borough delegations, not individual members. The council_member column carries both without distinguishing them. Anyone aggregating "awards per member" from this field would silently fold delegation money into a member count. Verified 2026-07-27; the older script said "41 distinct council members," which is not quite right.

Sharpen the pattern for them: the interesting output is not the match, it's the mismatch — allocations with no corresponding spending, or spending exceeding allocation. This makes that comparison cheap enough to run across every organization rather than the three you already suspected. Which is exactly what Act 5 takes away.

⚠️ Query by sponsoring member surname, never district. council_district=10 once returned $47.5M of citywide awards; it's now rejected by name in budget MCP 1.3.0.

Act 3 — the strongest capability act. And read the warning in Act 6 before you quote these numbers.

"Adopted budgets get amended all year by transparency resolution, and rescissions carry negative amounts. This is money that was announced, then quietly withdrawn. A transfer shows up as a rescind and a designate on the same EIN, so the tool can distinguish 'moved' from 'taken away' — precisely the distinction a press release will not make."

The unattributed rows are the thing to point at. 345 of 1,291 rescissions, $176.6 million, with no council member on the row. The largest is $12.5M to DSS/HRA. Act 6 The act for this room

Published, and machine-hostile.

›

What comes back HTTP 403

  • total_records: 0a count
  • records: []an empty set
  • has_more: falseand a claim of completeness
  • error: "HTTP 403" — in the same payload

Read the count and not the error, and an outage becomes a finding.

What we know

  • The site loads fine in a browser, on the same machinecurl gets 403; a Chrome User-Agent doesn't help
  • Site-wide, not /api-specificIncapsula JavaScript challenge
  • Covers the documented XML API, not just searchworked 2026-07-16, broken by 2026-07-21
  • Filed against our own connector, not worked aroundnyc-checkbook-mcp#21

The data is not secret. It is published and unreachable by a program.

Act 5 — the wall is the point. Three minutes of real attention.

"The site is up. I can pull this record by hand in a browser right now, and I will if you want. What I cannot do is reach it programmatically."

"Notice the shape of the failure. A record count of zero, an empty result set, and an error field, all in the same response. If you read the count and not the error, the honest answer 'we could not reach Checkbook' becomes the false answer 'this organization received no city money.' That is a public-data outage that reads as a finding."

What the block actually is, re-verified 2026-07-27. The clean comparison is one source address, two clients, same minute: a real browser loads the site normally (200), and the command-line client on that same machine gets 403 on the documented API. So the discriminator is the client, not the network. A separate datacenter address was stopped earlier still, at the edge, with a different block page, so IP reputation is a second and independent gate.

"It isn't that we can't reach them. It's that a documented XML API requires a browser to call it, which is a contradiction in terms."

⚠️ We have not spoofed headers or tried to defeat the bot protection, and we are not going to. Say that plainly. The route to public data is the front door.

⚠️ Keep this act descriptive, not an ask. Report the failure shape and stop. Do not turn it into advocacy directed at another office, and do not solicit anything from the room on it. If someone asks what we are doing about it, say we have raised it directly and are waiting.

Act 7 And the thing we got wrong

We found this in our own
tool this morning.

What our tool reported

$122.3M
500 rescissions
  • Header reads 500 row(s):the same shape a complete result would print
  • No total, no has_more, no warningyou cannot tell 500 from 500-of-1,291
  • Passing limit: 5000 returns the same 500silently clamped

Understated by $90,314,887 and 791 rows.

What is actually there

$212.6M
1,291 rescissions
  • resolution is TEXT, so it sorts "1", "10", "2"truncation isn't even chronological
  • Resolutions 3 through 9 entirely absent — $60.3M
  • Filed as New-York-City-Budget#422026-07-27, with the fix proposed

Real, correctly formatted, incomplete data. No error.

This is the close. Do not soften it.

What happened. Preparing this deck, we re-ran the rescission query. The tool returned exactly 500 rows against a limit of 500 — the truncation signature our own guide tells presenters to watch for. Raising the limit to 5,000 returned the identical 500 rows, because cap() clamps to 500 with no signal.

"The number I would have shown you was $122 million. The real number is $212 million. Our tool was ninety million dollars low, and it told me nothing was wrong."

The part that matters most to this room. This is the second time this exact failure shipped into a demo script, and the second one was inside the fix for the first. The earlier version was truncated at 50 rows; the correction said "re-run at limit 500 and read the real total off the result." That remediation is itself capped.

"We wrote the rule — treat exactly-N-against-a-limit-of-N as truncation — and then broke it in the fix for the last time we broke it. I'd rather you hear that from us."

Then the point of the act, which is not an ask. The reason to show them our own bug is that it is the same failure class as everything else in the deck: a tool returning real, correctly formatted, incomplete data with no error. We are not exempt from the thing we are teaching.

"You have more standing on this than we do. If a request like that carries more weight with your name next to it, we would welcome that."
