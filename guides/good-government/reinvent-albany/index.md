## What the session covers

Every other script here demos to someone who wants to see **their own** data. This audience wants to
see **what nobody has assembled yet**, which inverts the whole design. Coverage gaps stop being
caveats to manage and become the content.

- **One vendor across the whole city.** 54 discretionary awards, 41 separate sponsors, $6,455,750,
  and no single line item anywhere that says so.
- **Allocation to disbursement**, and the point where the join stops working — the budget code gets
  you to a real check but not to *whose* allocation it discharges.
- **What got taken back.** 1,291 FY2026 rescissions worth $212,600,875, of which **345 rows carry no
  council member at all**.
- **A report the law requires annually** whose most recent published version is from 2019–2020.
- **The reform graveyard**, the act built for this organization specifically: the same bill across
  four sessions and three sponsors over fourteen years, none of it becoming law, indexed nowhere.

## What it is honest about

Two acts are about BetaNYC being wrong.

We published a rescission table that was truncated at a silent row limit, then published a
correction telling presenters to pass `limit: 500` — **which is itself the cap**. The real figure was
$90,314,887 higher than what we had shown. Both breaks are on a slide.

And when we reported that a public API was blocking us, the office answered in two days, fixed two
catalog entries, corrected one of our findings, and the residual block turned out to be **ours** for
exceeding an undocumented rate limit. That is on a slide too.
