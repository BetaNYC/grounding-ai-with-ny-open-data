This page lists the changes to Grounding AI with NY Open Data that a reader would notice: new guides, demo scripts and slide decks, new data in the directory of New York's data portals, and corrections when we published something wrong. Newest first.

  • History begins: 2026-07-06, the day the repository started on GitHub. The website opened on 2026-07-30; entries before that date describe the guides as they were on GitHub. Every correction we know of since 2026-07-06 is listed.
  • Data refresh: none on a schedule. The directory of data portals and the figures in the guides are checked by hand, and each guide says the date its figures were run.

To report a mistake or ask for a new feature, use the feedback page or open an issue on GitHub.

Research archives in the data directory now say what they hold

November 13, 2026

Tags: Data, Correction

Earlier today the directory said its 15 new research archives all hold government or public data. A visit to each archive in a web browser found that seven mostly hold papers and PDF reports, or copies of national or global data. They stay listed so you know where the information is, and their notes now start "Papers, not data" (CUNY Academic Works, both SUNY archives, Binghamton's and ESF's) or "Not New York data" (UAlbany's weather server and Columbia's climate forecasts). Columbia's data platform row listed New York examples that the browser visit could not find; it now lists only what was seen, and gives its size, 497 datasets. The Roper Center row now warns that Roper's rules do not allow its survey data in AI tools. NYU's Faculty Digital Archive was down during the visit and is marked for a recheck.

If you used the old numbers: Do not use Roper Center survey data to ground an AI tool. Do not expect the seven archives above to hold datasets. Do not cite Columbia's data platform for New York jobs, housing-survey or drinking-water data.

Who it affects: Anyone who used the directory's research archive rows on 2026-11-13.

Every New York city, and 16 research archives, in the directory of data portals

November 13, 2026

Tags: Data

The directory now lists 224 sources, up from 160. All 62 cities in New York now have a row and a details page. The 48 that were missing were each checked on their own website on 2026-11-13. Only Lockport publishes open data (spending spreadsheets). 43 have no data site, though nine of them, including Niagara Falls and Rome, share public map layers such as tax parcels. Four sites (Hudson, Oneonta, Rye and Tonawanda) could not be read and are marked "Not yet confirmed". Also new: 15 university, library and research-center archives that hold government or public data, such as NYU's Faculty Digital Archive and Cornell's eCommons, and Columbia's data platform. Each archive row says what data it holds, and its region is the area that data covers.

Eight portal links went to another state

November 5, 2026

Tags: Data, Correction

In the list of New York open data portals, eight county rows linked to a portal in another state with a similar name, from July 6 to November 5, 2026: Delaware, Greene, Monroe and Seneca linked to Ohio, Franklin to Virginia, Montgomery to Maryland, Orange to California and Wyoming to Pennsylvania. All eight are fixed, and Monroe now links to its own New York portal. The fix also removed alternate links to out-of-state portals from the Wayne County row (Michigan and Ohio) and the Columbia County row (Wisconsin). Every link in the list was checked again on November 5.

If you used the old numbers: If you used the list for any of those eight counties before November 5, 2026, the data you found came from the other state. If you followed an alternate link for Wayne or Columbia County, it also went to another state. Use the links in the list now.

Who it affects: Anyone who used the portal list for those ten counties.

Two decks misdescribed Admin Code § 2-102, and one understated BetaNYC's FY2026 Council funding

October 19, 2026

Tags: Site, Data, Correction

The CUNY liberal arts deck, from July 29, 2026, and the AI Civics community of practice deck, from September 18, 2026, said Administrative Code section 2-102 names the official animals of New York City. It describes the City seal. The CUNY deck's closing slide also listed one of BetaNYC's own Council awards for FY2026, $25,000, where BetaNYC's FY2026 Council funding was $190,000 in all; it now lists all of it.

If you used the old numbers: If you repeated the section 2-102 example from either deck, it is about the City seal, not an official animal. If you used the $25,000 figure as BetaNYC's FY2026 Council funding, use $190,000. That total cannot be found as one line: $140,000 is in the Council's discretionary funding disclosure, and the other $50,000 is BetaNYC's share of a $208,000 line for the Artificial Intelligence Community Engagement initiative, filed under the Fund for the City of New York with no Council Member named.

Who it affects: Anyone who saw or reused these two decks between July 29 and October 19, 2026.

Two Checkbook claims in the Reinvent Albany material were wrong

July 30, 2026

Tags: Data, Correction

From July 21 to July 30, 2026, the Reinvent Albany script said a bot-protection layer in front of checkbooknyc.com blocked automated requests, and that a prime-expense registration date was not exposed through Checkbook's public API. The block that remained after the office's fix was BetaNYC's own, for going over an unpublished limit of one request per second, fixed in nyc-checkbook-mcp 1.6.0. The Comptroller's office told us the registration date is exposed, as a response field.

If you used the old numbers: Do not say the City or the Comptroller blocked BetaNYC's access to Checkbook, and do not say the registration date is unavailable. The one-request-per-second limit is not published on checkbooknyc.com, and the 20,000-record limit appears only on the API landing page. The Comptroller's office stated both on July 28, 2026.

Who it affects: Anyone who used the Reinvent Albany script or repeated its account of Checkbook access.

Wrong figures in the Council district and Reinvent Albany demos

July 21, 2026

Tags: Data, Correction

For about 12 hours on July 21, 2026, the demo scripts for Council Districts 4 and 10 gave discretionary funding totals that were too low: District 4 had 77 awards, $1,538,000, not 40 awards, $1,148,000; District 10 had 105 awards, $2,165,000, not 12 awards, $960,000. The District 4 script also said Council Member Keith Powers "does not appear at all" in the funding data; in fact the answer depends on the fiscal year. In the Reinvent Albany script, the list of FY2026 budget rescissions stopped at 50 rows, partway through the alphabet.

If you used the old numbers: Use 77 awards and $1,538,000 for District 4, 105 awards and $2,165,000 for District 10, and 4 awards and $90,000, not 2 and $50,000, for District 10's digital equity awards. In July, searching the FY2026 funding data for "Powers" returned both Keith Powers and Council Member Brooks-Powers, summed into one total; for FY2027 it returned only Brooks-Powers. The Reinvent Albany rescissions list was not the full FY2026 set, so do not use a copy of it as complete. When a count equals the number of rows you asked for, ask for more before you trust it.

Who it affects: Anyone who used the District 4, District 10 or Reinvent Albany scripts on July 21, 2026.
